# FindingMAGIC on the Road — Project Overview

## What this is
A UW MHCI capstone project sponsored by the Google Maps team. Team name: Smooooth Operators. Reimagining road trip navigation for travelers aged 18 to 24 using Gemini AI. Scope covers research, design, and a functional prototype.

## Core problems
1. Navigation tools are solo experiences that exclude passengers
2. Planning stress keeps travelers from being present
3. Current tools lack delight and surprise

## Design direction
Shift Google Maps from an individual driver navigation tool to a product that supports a shared group emotional experience across pre trip, during trip, and post trip phases.

## Four priority research insights
1. AI lacks human credibility. Travelers trust real people and real reviews over AI when planning. P4, 24, trusts a person's recommendation "100 times over AI."
2. Google Maps treats the car as a single user, when it is actually a group. Coordinating spontaneous stops breaks the driver's flow. P3, 22, driver, does not want the map to shut off entirely during coordination.
3. AI fails at fast, nuanced group decisions. It works for objective problems like a dead car key fix, but not fluid choices like where to eat. P4, 24, says explaining a group situation to AI again becomes cumbersome.
4. Tools miss the human connection that outlasts the trip. The lasting value is the bond, not just the memory. P6, 23: "the memories are great... but it's like the sprinkles, whereas the connections are the ice cream."

## The MAGIC framework
Across interviews, magic on a road trip shows up when three things align: human connection, novelty, and the surrounding environment. Magic is not the place itself, it is the shared experience, timing, people, and story attached to it.

## Trip phases and HMWs
- Pre trip: How might we bring human review context from across platforms into Google Maps so travelers can make informed decisions in one place?
- During trip: How might we help travelers make spontaneous stops and discover hidden gems while keeping the group aligned?
- Post trip: How might we help travelers capture and remember feelings and details without pulling them out of the moment?

## Prototype
Built in SwiftUI using the Gemini API and Google Maps APIs.

## Working principles
- Insights are credible when tied to specific quotes and behavioral episodes, not general opinions
- Interviews anchor to a specific trip or memory before going general, tool and AI questions come last
- Behavioral evidence beats stated preferences or feature validation
